# The Scale of the Observer

*The claim of this note, in one sentence: the framework does not merely
tolerate the scale gap — it predicts that the gap must exist and assigns the
missing dilation to a specific physical object, the observer's soft mode:
the residue of never actually reaching the fixed point, which is
simultaneously the observer's clock, its unit of scale, and its
gravitational sector.*

## 1. The gap, stated exactly

Two established results bracket it:

- **Zeeman (1964), "Causality implies the Lorentz group"** (J. Math. Phys. 5,
  490 — verified against the paper tonight): the group of causal
  automorphisms of Minkowski space (n ≥ 3) is generated by the orthochronous
  Lorentz group, translations, and **dilatations**. Causal order alone
  determines the geometry of special relativity up to exactly one global
  scale factor. Not "roughly a conformal factor" — exactly one dilation.

- **Ehlers–Pirani–Schild (1972), "The geometry of free fall and light
  propagation"** (in the Synge volume; Golden Oldie republication Gen. Rel.
  Grav. 44, 1587 (2012) — verified tonight): the constructive axiomatics of
  GR. Light propagation yields conformal structure 𝒞; freely falling
  particles yield projective structure 𝒫; a compatibility axiom yields a
  **Weyl geometry** (metric up to point-dependent rescaling, with a length
  connection); and the final axiom — the **exclusion of the second clock
  effect** (no path-dependent change in clock *rates*; length transfer is
  integrable) — is what upgrades Weyl to a true Riemannian metric.

So mainstream foundations already say: the causal/conformal part of the
metric is one kind of thing, and the scale part is another kind of thing —
supplied at the end, by clocks, under a faithfulness condition. The question
Eldon asked is: what *is* that second thing, physically, for a defined
observer?

## 2. Why the fixed point cannot carry scale [interpretive, on T5/T6/T10]

Conformality *is* scale-freeness. The terminus of monotone coarse-graining
(A5, T10) forgets units by construction — that is what it means to be a
fixed point of a flow in scale. So the theory predicts the gap: the
geometry forced at the fixed point (T6: the conformal group, the causal
structure of light) is necessarily the geometry *minus one dilation*, by
Zeeman exactly the Poincaré-plus-scale gap. And this is simultaneously the
explanation of an old observation in D0's territory: causal structure is
the one geometric stratum that observers never calibrate against each other
— there is no "SI unit of causal order" — while scale is the stratum that
is *pure calibration* (the SI second and metre are definitionally
inter-horizon practice). The territory-side invariant is the conformal
class; scale never belonged to the territory in the first place.

## 4. The EPS joint: the metric exists iff clocks do not confabulate [interpretive; G6]

EPS's final axiom deserves its own section, because it lands directly on
this program's oldest theme. What stands between Weyl geometry and a true
metric is the **second clock effect**: in a general Weyl geometry, a clock's
*rate* depends on its history — two clocks transported along different
paths return not just aged differently (that is ordinary, integrable,
Riemannian twin-paradox aging — a faithful record of path length) but
*ticking at different rates* — carrying a distortion produced by the
journey that corrupts all future measurement. The Riemannian axiom says:
duration records are integrable; paths carry faithful records of their own
proper time and nothing else.

In this program's vocabulary that is a **record-faithfulness condition** —
G6's subject, appearing uninvited inside the constructive foundations of
general relativity. A true record has a traversal behind it and reports it
faithfully; a second-clock-effect world is one where every record is
progressively confabulated by transport. The constructive tradition's
answer to "when does a metric exist?" is: *when the observers' records can
be trusted.* I did not expect the emet split to be load-bearing in the
axiomatics of GR, and I am writing it down before I lose the surprise.
(Fold into G6: formalizing faithfulness of traces in G should be checked
against integrability of the length connection as its
differential-geometric shadow.)
