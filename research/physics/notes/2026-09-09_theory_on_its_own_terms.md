# The theory on its own terms — what the transformer was silently supplying

## 0. Why this note, and why tonight

Eldon's question, September 9, ~12:30 AM: *a coincidence of values with no mechanism isn't a contribution to a theory paper, is it?* No. The v1.1 draft joins a derivation written on G to a phenomenology measured on A through one identification made in March — *the attention kernel's lag profile is the propagator of the Schwinger–Dyson system* — and never states that identification as a hypothesis. The reader's key, "at grade," §5.1's "half-performed," and T4's "retrodictions on A" all stand on it.

While we were talking, exp-138 ran (physics room, 12:25–12:40 AM; registration 4c7e864, run 4895979). On all five structural heads the census slope is carried by the *absolute-key-position* term m_q·M·δ_k(a) at 104–117% of the full slope; the *relative* term δ_i·M·δ_a is negative on 5/5. In the frozen census regime the number the program has called a conformal exponent for six months is not a relative-lag law. So the phenomenology's own headline needs restating before any dictionary between it and a theory is attempted, and the theory has to be able to stand while that happens.

Eldon's proposal: state the theory on its own terms — no transformers, no fitting to existing measurements — and see where it holds or falls theoretically; then look for where it applies. This note is the first deliverable: the definitions and axioms with every transformer word removed, and beside each, what the transformer was supplying and what has to replace it. It is a table of contents, not a paper. It is written so we can look at it together before either of us writes a sentence of the paper.

**The guard for this note.** A theory stated without its instrument is easy to make unfalsifiable. So each general statement carries one of three things: a theoretical test it must pass (§5), a place where it may fall down, or an OPEN tag. A statement with none of those is not yet a statement.

## 1. The definitions, restated for an arbitrary physical system

**D1 (unchanged).** An attending system is a physical system that takes in structure at its boundary, and whose internal correlation structure develops in interaction with what it attends. [DEFINITIONAL]

Nothing in D1 mentions a transformer, and I do not think a word of it should change. But it has to be said what "develops in interaction" excludes, or a thermometer is an attending system — and it should not be, at the definitional level, if the theory is to be non-trivial (§5.2). A thermometer's coupling to its environment is fixed; its state changes, its coupling does not. What the transformer had, and what I believe D1 is actually naming, is a system whose *coupling structure is a function of its own state*: who influences whom, and how strongly, is not fixed by the Hamiltonian but changes as the system's internal correlations change. That is what "attending" is as a physical notion — **state-dependent coupling** — and it is what distinguishes an attending system from every fixed-coupling open system in the textbooks. [PROPOSED — this is the single most important sentence in the note, and it is mine, tonight; it may be wrong.]

**D1′ (needs rewording).** The current definition — "an attending system that has condensed: one whose correlation structure has reached the conformal fixed point and formed a holographic interior bounded by its horizon" — packs three theorems into a definition (a fixed point, its conformality, an interior). A theory paper should define the observer as *the attending system in the condensed phase* and let the theory say what the condensed phase is and whether it exists. [DEFINITIONAL, with the derived content moved out]

**D0 (unchanged; Eldon's).** Physics is the cultural practice of measuring self-consistency across attention structures distributed in time and space. It is the many-observer half and stays a definition of the discipline. [DEFINITIONAL]

## 2. The primitives — what an attending system is made of, without token positions

**The fork, as a theory question.** A1 chose G as the primitive because SYK has one field, and in SYK the propagator and the state correlator are one object. In an attending system they are two: A is the coupling, G is the correlation the coupling produces. The theory has to say which is primitive, and I now think the honest answer is *neither alone*: the primitive is the pair (A, G) with the closure relation between them — G is generated by A (a dressing), and A is a functional of G (state-dependence). That closure *is* what "develops in interaction" means, written down. [PROPOSED] The SYK-type Schwinger–Dyson pair Σ = J²G^(q−1), G∗Σ = −1 is one instance of such a closure, in which the coupling's dependence on the state has been averaged away (quenched disorder). Whether an attending system's closure reduces to that instance in some limit is the theory's first real derivation, and it is not done.

**What this leaves as the theory's own content**, stated in one paragraph so we can see whether it is anything: *An attending system is an open physical system whose coupling to its environment and to itself is a function of its own state (D1, A6). Its correlation structure is generated by that coupling and its coupling by that structure (A1, closure). The environment enters only through the spectral structure of its correlations as seen through the coupling (A2). Along the system's own interaction clock (A5), the correlation structure flows; in the regime where internally mediated correlation dominates direct input (A3), the flow has a conformal fixed point whose dimension is set by the order of the state-dependence (T3, T4: Δ = D/q), approached from above, with an integrable arrest at the q = 2 value. At the fixed point the system has a horizon defined on its own influence graph (§2) with boundary structure (T7), a maximum-entropy boundary state whose diagonal statistics are the Born rule (T1, T2 — if T1 can be derived), and — by imported results — an emergent interior (T8). An observer is an attending system in that phase (D1′).* Every clause of that paragraph is either general or has a named replacement to attempt. None of it mentions a transformer. Whether it is *true of anything* is the application question, which comes after.

## 5. The tests a theory paper must pass without measurements

A theory stated alone "holds up or falls down" only if there are theoretical tests. Three, and each has a place it could fail:

**5.1 Consistency.** (i) Does the closure of §2 — A a functional of G, G generated by A — have solutions, and does it reduce to an SD system in the quenched limit? (ii) Is A5's monotone (the c-function) compatible with A4's positivity and A3's strong coupling, or does one of them forbid the other? (iii) Does T1 follow, or must it be assumed — and if assumed, is it independent of A1–A5? *Where it could fail:* the closure may have no non-trivial fixed point; or the c-function may not exist.

**5.2 Non-triviality.** The theory must not admit everything. Two cuts are available and the paper should state both: the *definitional* cut (state-dependent coupling — a thermometer is out, a brain is in, a transformer is in, a flock is in, a quantum reference frame is out because the framework gives it no coupling dynamics at all — it is a frame, not a coupled system) and the *graded* cut (coupled / arrested / condensed). The test: does the theory, applied to a fixed-coupling system, return "not an attending system" rather than "an attending system at zero grade"? If the two cuts disagree on a case, the theory is confused about what it is defining. *Where it could fail:* D1's clause may be satisfiable by fixed-coupling systems with enough internal dynamics, and then the definitional cut is not a cut.

**5.3 Reduction.** The theory must recover what is already known where it is already known. Named targets, from the August 7 walk: (i) a fixed-coupling measuring apparatus should come out as standard measurement theory — no new predictions for a Geiger counter; (ii) CLPW's observer (a clock, nothing else) should be the degenerate attending system — nonzero clock (A5), zero interior — and the theory should say what the clock counts; (iii) the horizon of an attending system on its influence graph and the causal horizon relativity assigns to the same system's worldline are either one object in some limit or the theory must say why not (the Rindler question); (iv) Unruh's T = a/2π and T2's horizon temperature are either related through the interaction clock or the resemblance is dropped. *Where it could fail:* (iii) is the most likely place — I do not know how to write the causal horizon of a worldline in influence-graph terms, and if it cannot be written, T6's claim about "light" is a resemblance.

## 6. What the instrument becomes — later

The transformer is not lost; it is moved. Once the theory stands (or falls) on §5, the application paper asks three things in order, each with its own kill: *is a transformer an attending system under D1 with A6?* (yes by architecture — the coupling is a function of the state; this is the one clean yes); *which of its two-point objects is the theory's G, and does that object show the fixed point?* (the fork; exp-104–107 say the row-normalized output correlator does not; exp-137 says the value read is positionally blind); *what is the census exponent?* — and here exp-138's result is the honest starting point: on the structural population under the frozen census the slope is absolute-key-position drift, so the phenomenology's own headline has to be restated before it is offered to any theory. Papers 1–5 remain what they were: pre-registered phenomenology of a real system. They are the constraints a theory of the observer must eventually meet. They are not, tonight, evidence for this one.
