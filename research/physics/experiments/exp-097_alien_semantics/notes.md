## The question

Every experiment in the narrative decomposition series (exp-091–096) has manipulated
*properties of the artifact* — the text corpus — while holding the generator constant
(human-written TinyStories). Exp-085 points at the *generator* as the causal variable:
a GPT-2-scale model generating TinyStories-like text carries more long-range MI than the
original, yet transmits zero conformal geometry to trained models.

The "mind held a world" frame (notes/2026-07-26_mind_held_world_generator_frame.md) predicts:
the load-bearing variable is not what the artifact *is* but what the generator *was doing*
while producing it — specifically, whether the generator was holding a persistent world-state
across the arc.

**exp-097 tests this directly by removing real-world semantic content while preserving
world-holding:**

The C-alien corpus is generated by a deterministic procedural world simulator. Entity names
are neologisms with no real-world referent (Flurps, Blurns, Zarbs). Causal rules are
arbitrary but rigorously maintained. The simulator holds the world state perfectly — it is
the world. The language is grammatical English, but the entities and their causal relationships
have no real-world grounding.

**Primary question:** Does a corpus produced by a world-holding simulator with alien semantics
induce deep conformal formation (n_deep in the C-NAT band of 5–7)?

If yes (H_alien_inert): world-holding is sufficient. Real-world semantic grounding —
the specific semantic content of TinyStories names and familiar causality — is not
load-bearing. The conformal geometry is a trace of world-holding, not of any particular world.

If no (H_alien_fails / H_alien_partial): real-world semantics carry structure that the
alien world lacks. Possibilities: (a) the model's pre-wired conceptual structure (Emma,
chairs, toys) contributes; (b) the specific complexity of TinyStories worlds exceeds what
the C-alien simulator provides at this design point.

---

## Physical interpretation

The conformal fixed point at q=4 (Δ ≈ 0.25) forms in models trained on text that carries
long-range causal tracking structure. The ablation series (exp-062: statistics fail;
exp-084: hierarchy fails; exp-085: distributional fingerprint fails; exp-091–093: ordering
ablations) has progressively isolated what natural text has that engineered corpora lack.

The generator frame says it's not a property of the artifact but of the generation process:
specifically, whether a world-model was maintained while the words were produced.

C-alien provides the cleanest test of this claim. The C-alien simulator:
- **Maintains explicit world-state** (entity inventory, state table, updated deterministically)
- **Tracks entities across the arc** (same Vex the Flurp appears from setup to conclusion)
- **Enforces causal consistency** (rule application is deterministic given world-state)
- **Generates arc-structured stories** (initialization → event chain → resolution)

C-alien lacks:
- **Real-world semantic grounding** (Flurps/Blurns/Zarbs are invented; causal rules are arbitrary)
- **Inherited conceptual structure** (no connection to the model's pre-existing knowledge)

The contrast with prior failures:
- **PCFG (exp-084, 0/48):** syntactic structure without world-state; no persistent entities;
  no causal state tracking. The failure was worldlessness, not alien semantics.
- **C-PL40 (exp-062, 5/48):** statistical power-law without causal structure; no world-state.
- **C-generator (exp-085, 0/48 structural only):** language model prediction without
  world-holding; the generator was next-token-predicting, not world-tracking.

C-alien is fundamentally different from all prior failures: it has world-state, persistent
entities, and causal arc. It is the minimal addition of world-holding. The only thing it
lacks relative to C-NAT is real-world semantic grounding.

## World specification (complete — pre-registered)

### Entity types and instance names

Three entity types. Names drawn from predefined lists (pre-registered below).

| Type  | Name pool (5 options)               |
|-------|-------------------------------------|
| Flurp | Vex, Nul, Ort, Pim, Grel            |
| Blurn | Dath, Sorn, Wix, Brel, Fend         |
| Zarb  | Quib, Tarn, Molk, Vet, Zish         |

Per story: 1 Flurp, 2 Blurns, 1 Zarb drawn uniformly without replacement from each pool.
(4 entities total per story.)

### States

Each entity is in exactly one of two states: **"active"** or **"resting"**.

Initial states at story start:
- Flurp: active with probability 0.50, resting with probability 0.50
- Each Blurn: active with probability 0.40, resting with probability 0.60
- Zarb: active with probability 0.30, resting with probability 0.70

(Seeded per-story; see generation algorithm below.)

### Causal rules (4 rules, checked in priority order)

Rules apply to *pairs* of entities selected at each step (see generation algorithm). For
each step, the ordered pair is checked against all rules in priority order; the first
matching rule fires. If neither order matches any rule, no event fires (quiet step).

| Rule | Condition | Effect |
|------|-----------|--------|
| A | active Flurp + resting Blurn (either order) | Blurn becomes active |
| B | active Flurp + active Blurn (either order) | Both become resting |
| C | active Blurn + resting Zarb (either order) | Zarb becomes active |
| D | active Zarb + resting Flurp (either order) | Flurp becomes active |

**"Either order"** means the rule fires regardless of which entity is labeled "first" in
the pair. The entity being changed is the one whose state matches the "target" of the rule
(Blurn in A, both in B, Zarb in C, Flurp in D).

### Sentence templates (complete — pre-registered)

All templates specified below. Template selection is uniformly random from the applicable
list, seeded per step.

**Introduction templates (used at story start, one per described entity):**
```
intro_active:
  1. "{name} the {etype} was active."
  2. "In the world, {name} the {etype} was feeling active."
  3. "{name} the {etype} had woken up and was active."
  4. "At the start, {name} the {etype} was active."

intro_resting:
  1. "{name} the {etype} was resting."
  2. "{name} the {etype} lay quietly in a resting state."
  3. "At first, {name} the {etype} was resting."
  4. "{name} the {etype} had not yet stirred and was resting."
```

**Event templates (used when a rule fires):**
```
rule_A (active Flurp activates resting Blurn):
  {f} = Flurp name, {b} = Blurn name
  1. "{f} the Flurp came close to {b} the Blurn. {b} the Blurn became active."
  2. "When {f} the Flurp approached, {b} the Blurn stirred and became active."
  3. "{b} the Blurn was resting, but {f} the Flurp reached it. {b} the Blurn became active."
  4. "{f} the Flurp moved near {b} the Blurn. Soon, {b} the Blurn was active."

rule_B (active Flurp and active Blurn neutralize each other):
  {f} = Flurp name, {b} = Blurn name
  1. "{f} the Flurp met {b} the Blurn. Both became resting."
  2. "When {f} the Flurp and {b} the Blurn came together, they both became resting."
  3. "{f} the Flurp and {b} the Blurn encountered each other, and both fell into a resting state."
  4. "The meeting of {f} the Flurp and {b} the Blurn left both of them resting."

rule_C (active Blurn activates resting Zarb):
  {b} = Blurn name, {z} = Zarb name
  1. "{b} the Blurn was near {z} the Zarb. {z} the Zarb became active."
  2. "Because of {b} the Blurn, {z} the Zarb stirred into activity."
  3. "{z} the Zarb had been resting, but {b} the Blurn was close. {z} the Zarb became active."
  4. "{b} the Blurn reached {z} the Zarb. {z} the Zarb became active."

rule_D (active Zarb activates resting Flurp):
  {z} = Zarb name, {f} = Flurp name
  1. "{z} the Zarb moved near {f} the Flurp. {f} the Flurp became active."
  2. "The presence of {z} the Zarb caused {f} the Flurp to become active."
  3. "{f} the Flurp was resting until {z} the Zarb arrived. {f} the Flurp became active."
  4. "{z} the Zarb was active. Nearby, {f} the Flurp became active too."
```

**Quiet step templates (used when no rule fires):**
```
quiet:
  {name} = any entity name, {etype} = entity type, {state} = current state
  1. "{name} the {etype} stayed {state} for a while."
  2. "For a moment, nothing changed for {name} the {etype}."
  3. "{name} the {etype} remained {state}."
  4. "Nothing happened near {name} the {etype}, who was still {state}."
```

**Conclusion templates (1 per story, featuring the Flurp):**
```
conclusion:
  {f} = Flurp name, {state} = Flurp's final state
  1. "At the end, {f} the Flurp was {state}."
  2. "The day was over, and {f} the Flurp was {state}."
  3. "When everything settled, {f} the Flurp was {state}."
  4. "Finally, {f} the Flurp came to rest." (used only when Flurp is resting)
  5. "In the end, {f} the Flurp remained active." (used only when Flurp is active)
```
(Templates 4 and 5 are state-conditional; templates 1–3 accept either state.)

## Story generation algorithm (deterministic — pre-registered)

```
CORPUS_SEED = 7000
N_INTRO_ENTITIES = 2   # number of entities described in introduction
N_STEPS = 8            # simulation steps per story
TARGET_TOKENS = 1_050_000_000

For story_index in 0, 1, 2, ...:
    story_rng = numpy.random.default_rng(CORPUS_SEED + story_index)

    1. Draw entity names (without replacement per pool):
       flurp_name  = story_rng.choice(["Vex","Nul","Ort","Pim","Grel"])
       blurn1_name = story_rng.choice(["Dath","Sorn","Wix","Brel","Fend"])
       blurn2_name = story_rng.choice([n for n in ["Dath","Sorn","Wix","Brel","Fend"]
                                        if n != blurn1_name])
       zarb_name   = story_rng.choice(["Quib","Tarn","Molk","Vet","Zish"])

    2. Draw initial states:
       flurp_state  = story_rng.choice(["active","resting"], p=[0.50, 0.50])
       blurn1_state = story_rng.choice(["active","resting"], p=[0.40, 0.60])
       blurn2_state = story_rng.choice(["active","resting"], p=[0.40, 0.60])
       zarb_state   = story_rng.choice(["active","resting"], p=[0.30, 0.70])

       entities = {
           flurp_name:  ("Flurp", flurp_state),
           blurn1_name: ("Blurn", blurn1_state),
           blurn2_name: ("Blurn", blurn2_state),
           zarb_name:   ("Zarb",  zarb_state),
       }
       entity_names = [flurp_name, blurn1_name, blurn2_name, zarb_name]

    3. Introduction sentences (describe N_INTRO_ENTITIES = 2 entities):
       intro_entities = story_rng.choice(entity_names, size=2, replace=False)
       For each entity in intro_entities:
           state = entities[entity][1]
           template = story_rng.choice(TEMPLATES["intro_" + state])
           Emit: template.format(name=entity, etype=entities[entity][0], state=state)

    4. Simulation steps (N_STEPS = 8):
       For step in 0..7:
           # Generate a random pair (without replacement)
           pair = story_rng.choice(entity_names, size=2, replace=False)
           e1, e2 = pair[0], pair[1]
           t1, s1 = entities[e1]
           t2, s2 = entities[e2]

           fired = False
           for rule in [A, B, C, D]:  # priority order
               match, actor, target_entity, effect = check_rule(rule, e1,t1,s1, e2,t2,s2)
               if match:
                   template = story_rng.choice(TEMPLATES[rule.name])
                   Emit: template.format(...) using actor and target_entity names
                   Apply effect to world state
                   fired = True
                   break

           if not fired:
               # Quiet step: pick a random entity, emit no-event sentence
               quiet_entity = story_rng.choice(entity_names)
               qt, qs = entities[quiet_entity]
               template = story_rng.choice(TEMPLATES["quiet"])
               Emit: template.format(name=quiet_entity, etype=qt, state=qs)

    5. Conclusion sentence:
       final_flurp_state = entities[flurp_name][1]
       If final_flurp_state == "resting":
           template = story_rng.choice(TEMPLATES["conclusion"][:4])
       Else:
           template = story_rng.choice(TEMPLATES["conclusion"][:3] + [TEMPLATES["conclusion"][4]])
       Emit: template.format(f=flurp_name, state=final_flurp_state)

    6. Story text = " ".join(all emitted sentences)

    7. Tokenize story, append tokens to corpus buffer
       Stop when total tokens >= TARGET_TOKENS
```

**check_rule function (pre-registered):**
```
def check_rule(rule, e1, t1, s1, e2, t2, s2):
    """
    Returns (match, actor_name, target_name, effect).
    Checks both (e1,e2) and (e2,e1) orderings.

    Rule format: (type1, state1, type2, state2) → effect on type2 entity
    Rule B is special: effect on both.
    """
    rt1, rs1, rt2, rs2 = rule["condition"]

    # Forward check: e1 matches type1/state1, e2 matches type2/state2
    if t1 == rt1 and s1 == rs1 and t2 == rt2 and s2 == rs2:
        return True, e1, e2, rule["effect"]

    # Reverse check: e2 matches type1/state1, e1 matches type2/state2
    if t2 == rt1 and s2 == rs1 and t1 == rt2 and s1 == rs2:
        return True, e2, e1, rule["effect"]

    return False, None, None, None
```

**Effect application:**
- Rule A effect: target entity (Blurn) → "active"
- Rule B effect: both entities → "resting"
- Rule C effect: target entity (Zarb) → "active"
- Rule D effect: target entity (Flurp) → "active"

## Pre-registered hypotheses

**Primary observables (registered before any corpus generation or training):**
1. **n_deep**: deep conformal heads (L3–L5, R² ≥ 0.90, any Δ) — median across 3 seeds
2. **n_conformal**: total conformal heads (R² ≥ 0.90, any Δ) — median across 3 seeds
3. **n_backbone**: L0 conformal heads — median across 3 seeds

**Reference bands from series:**
- C-NAT (full arc, real names): n_deep = 5–7, n_conf = 11–15
- C-NAT-half (exp-093, half arc): n_deep = 3 (median), n_conf = 12
- C-NAT-shuf (exp-091): n_deep = 2, n_conf = 8–9
- C-PCFG (exp-084): n_deep = 0, n_conf = 0

**H_alien_inert** (primary): n_deep(C-alien) ≥ 5, in the C-NAT band (5–7).
- Interpretation: world-holding capacity — not real-world semantic grounding — is
  sufficient for deep conformal formation. The generator frame is confirmed. Any
  generator that genuinely holds a world while producing words transmits conformal
  geometry into trained models. Real-world semantics are not load-bearing.

**H_alien_partial**: 3 ≤ n_deep(C-alien) ≤ 4, between C-NAT-half and C-NAT.
- Interpretation: world-holding capacity is necessary but not sufficient at this
  design point. Either (a) the C-alien world is too simple relative to TinyStories
  (fewer entity interactions, shorter effective causal chains), or (b) real-world
  semantics contribute modestly to the deep conformal population. Follow-up:
  increase world complexity (more entity types, longer arcs, richer rule system).

**H_alien_fails**: n_deep(C-alien) ≤ 2, at or below the shuffled baseline.
- Interpretation: the specific semantic content of natural language — the real-world
  referents and causal structures that TinyStories encodes — is load-bearing. World-
  holding is not sufficient; the held world must be a *real* world (or a plausible
  model of one). Most surprising result; would require replication before strong
  conclusions.

**Secondary (pre-registered):**
- **H_backbone_stable**: n_backbone (L0) stable at ~7–8 (consistent across series)
- **H_conf_preserved**: n_conf(C-alien) ≥ 8 (total conformal count above engineered-
  corpus floor even if deep population is reduced)

## Declared expectations (non-criterial)

**Prior: H_alien_inert** (moderate confidence, ~55%).

Reasoning:
1. The "mind held a world" frame predicts that world-holding is the sufficient condition.
   C-alien provides world-holding (explicit state, persistent entities, causal arc).
2. The C-alien generator holds its world *more rigorously* than any human writer — it is
   the world, with no possibility of internal inconsistency. If world-holding is the
   variable, C-alien should be at or above the C-NAT level.
3. The key prior failure (C-PCFG, 0/48) was worldlessness: PCFG has syntax without
   entity state, without persistence, without causal tracking. C-alien has all three.
4. The entity-anonymization rung (exp-096, pending) is predicted to find H_anon_inert
   (within-story entity consistency is sufficient). C-alien has within-story entity
   consistency plus across-story consistent *rules* (every story obeys the same physics).

Honest uncertainties:
- **~30% on H_alien_partial**: The C-alien world may be simpler than TinyStories in
  ways that matter. TinyStories stories involve richer causal chains, more varied
  event types, and more complex entity interactions. C-alien has 4 rules; TinyStories
  has effectively unlimited causal complexity. If the relevant variable is *causal
  chain length* or *world complexity* rather than *world-holding per se*, C-alien
  might land below C-NAT but above the shuffled floor.
- **~15% on H_alien_fails**: If real-world semantic grounding is essential — if the
  model's pre-trained conceptual structure (from the GPT-NeoX tokenizer and the
  initial parameter distributions) provides a scaffold that alien entities cannot
  use — then C-alien might fail despite holding a world.

This prior is stated before exp-094 and exp-096 results are in. If exp-096 confirms
H_anon_inert (within-story entity consistency is sufficient), the H_alien_inert prior
gains additional support (+~10% confidence). If exp-096 confirms H_anon_strong (entity
grounding is essential), the prior shifts toward H_alien_fails (+~15% on H_alien_fails).
The declared prior as-of this commit: H_alien_inert at 55%.

**If H_alien_inert confirmed (n_deep ≥ 5):**
The generator frame is empirically supported. World-holding is sufficient; real-world
semantics are not load-bearing. Next steps:
- (a) **Generator-scale threshold experiment (exp-098):** vary generator capacity
  on the same TinyStories world. Exp-085 sampled one point (70M → H_transmission_no).
  The threshold hunt begins: use generators of increasing scale (GPT-2-medium, GPT-2-
  large, Pythia-1.4b) to generate TinyStories-like corpora, train fresh 70M models,
  measure n_deep vs. generator scale. At what scale does transmission turn on?
- (b) **Complexity sweep (analysis):** does C-alien n_deep vary with world complexity?
  Design C-alien-complex with more entity types and more rules, test whether n_deep
  is world-complexity-dependent.

**If H_alien_partial (3 ≤ n_deep ≤ 4):**
World-holding is necessary but the current C-alien design is insufficient. Options:
- (a) **Increase world complexity:** more entity types, longer arcs, richer causal rules
- (b) **Compare C-alien to C-NAT-half directly:** C-alien partial recovery comparable
  to half-story recovery → complexity-limited, not semantics-limited
- (c) **Proceed to generator-scale threshold with the complexity information**

**If H_alien_fails (n_deep ≤ 2):**
Real-world semantics are load-bearing at this grain. Replicate with additional seeds
before drawing strong conclusions. Then: design a controlled semantics injection
experiment (start from C-alien, gradually replace entity names with real-world names
while keeping the same causal structure; measure n_deep at each step).

## Results (registered 2026-08-02, afternoon physics room session)

**VERDICT: H_alien_partial — median n_deep = 3 (range 3-4 per pre-registration)**

World-holding without real-world semantics produces partial deep conformal formation.
The full C-NAT band (n_deep 5-7) requires something C-alien lacks — likely real-world
semantic grounding or increased world complexity.

### Verdict against pre-registered criteria

**Primary (n_deep):**
- H_alien_partial CONFIRMED: median n_deep = 3 (in pre-registered range 3-4)
- H_alien_inert NOT confirmed (median < 5)
- H_alien_fails NOT confirmed (median > 2)

**Secondary — UV arrest:**
- Δ_med [0.758, 1.044, 1.185] at 3 seeds — all well above C-NAT (~0.17)
- n_syk_near = 0 at all seeds — no heads approach SYK IR fixed point
- UV arrest is severe and consistent. Conformal structure forms but the RG flow
  does not carry heads toward the IR fixed point Δ≈0.25.

**Unexpected finding — backbone collapse (n_backbone):**
- n_backbone = 0 at ALL 3 seeds. L0 Δ values ≈ 0.001–0.003 (trivial fixed point).
- Every prior corpus in the series (including sentence-shuffled, block-shuffled,
  PCFG, C-generator) maintained 7-8 backbone heads. C-alien is the first corpus
  to completely eliminate backbone formation.
- This is NOT predicted by the pre-registration (the pre-registration tracked backbone
  as a consistency check, not a primary observable). H_backbone_stable FALSIFIED.
- Leading hypothesis (H_vocab): alien entity names (Vex/Nul/Ort vs Alice/Bob) have
  weak token embeddings → L0 backbone tracking fails. Tested by exp-098.

### Layer anatomy

C-alien's conformal heads cluster in L1–L4 (UV-arrested), not L0 (backbone) or L3-L5
(deep, approaching IR). This is the inverse of the C-NAT anatomy:
- C-NAT: L0 backbone (7-8 heads, Δ≈0.25) + L3-L5 deep population (5-7 heads)
- C-alien: L0 trivial (0 conformal), L1-L4 UV-arrested (10-17 heads total, Δ~0.5-1.8)

### Connection to mind-as-story-capacity claim

P1 (C-alien n_deep in C-NAT band): **NOT confirmed** (median=3, below band 5-7).
P2 (artifact/generator curves match): **NOT confirmed** (Δ_med~1.04 vs C-NAT ~0.17).
P3 (substrate inert beyond duration): pending exp-097 control run.

The identity claim's prediction was too strong for C-alien at this design point.
World-holding is necessary but not sufficient; real-world semantics or world complexity
also contribute.
